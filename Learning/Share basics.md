### Core Concepts Covered

#### 1. Stock Market Basics
- A **share** represents a **fractional ownership** in a company, similar to owning a part of a land divided into pieces.
- Companies raise funds by issuing shares to investors, who become part-owners and can earn profits if the company grows.
- **Angel investors** provide early-stage capital against a percentage of company ownership.
- Further funding rounds (Series A, B, C, etc.) occur for expansion.
- An **IPO (Initial Public Offering)** is when a company goes public by offering shares to the general public, transitioning from a private limited to a public company.

#### 2. Stock Exchanges and Indexes
- In India, the two main exchanges are:
  - **NSE (National Stock Exchange)**
  - **BSE (Bombay Stock Exchange)**
- **Index**: A group of stocks representing a market segment.
  - **NIFTY 50**: Top 50 companies by market capitalization.
  - **SENSEX**: Top 30 companies.
- Sector-specific indexes include NIFTY IT, NIFTY Bank, etc.

#### 3. Demat and Trading Accounts
- **Demat Account**: Digital account to hold shares electronically, replacing physical share certificates.
- **Trading Account**: Used to buy or sell shares; linked to a Demat account.
- Investors use brokers (e.g., Zerodha, Upstox) who facilitate opening these accounts and execute trades on their behalf.

### Fundamental Analysis (FA)
- Evaluates a company’s intrinsic value based on financial health and business prospects.
- Key pillars:
  - **Profit & Loss Statement (P&L)**: How much profit or loss is generated.
  - **Balance Sheet**: Assets, liabilities, and debts of the company.
  - **Company-related news and market environment**.
  
- Example Comparison:  
| Metric               | Reliance Industries          | Ola                        |
|----------------------|------------------------------|----------------------------|
| Profit (Last 5 years) | Consistent growth (₹53,739 Cr to ₹81,309 Cr) | Negative profits & increasing debt |
| Debt                 | ~₹7 lakh crore (used for expansion)           | Rising debt, causing losses  |
| Conclusion           | Reliance considered strong company; Ola weaker financially |

- **Important Financial Ratios**:  
| Ratio               | Definition                                                  | Good Indicator            |
|---------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------|
| EPS (Earnings Per Share)   | Profit attributable per share                               | Higher EPS is better       |
| P/E Ratio (Price-to-Earnings) | Share price relative to EPS                                  | Lower P/E generally better |
| Debt-to-Equity Ratio       | Degree of financial leverage (debt reliance)                | Lower ratio preferred      |
| ROE (Return on Equity)     | % return generated on shareholders’ equity                  | Higher ROE is better       |
| Market Cap                 | Total market value of all shares                             | Indicates company size     |
| Book Value                 | Net asset value per share                                    | Close price to book = good |
| Price-to-Book Ratio (P/B)  | Market price relative to book value                          | Lower P/B ratio preferred  |

- Reliable companies show consistent profit growth, manageable debt, positive news, and good ratios.

### Technical Analysis (TA)

#### Candlestick Basics
- A **candlestick** displays price movements with four data points:  
  - **Open (O)**: Price at start of the period  
  - **High (H)**: Highest price reached  
  - **Low (L)**: Lowest price reached  
  - **Close (C)**: Price at end of the period  
- Color-coded (Green for price rise, Red for fall)
- Wick (or shadow): Lines that show price extremes.
- Patterns from candlesticks predict future price movements.

#### Single Candlestick Patterns
- **Hammer**: Indicates potential trend reversal after a downtrend; bullish sign.
- **Hanging Man**: Appears at an uptrend, signals possible reversal downward.
- **Inverted Hammer** and **Shooting Star**: Reversal indicators with wicks on top.
- **Doji**: Open and close prices are equal/nearly equal; signals indecision.

#### Multiple Candlestick Patterns
- **Engulfing Pattern**: One candle fully engulfs the prior; signals strong reversal.
  - Bullish Engulfing (after a downtrend)
  - Bearish Engulfing (after an uptrend)
- **Harami**: Smaller candle inside the prior larger body; indicates possible reversal.
- **Piercing** and **Dark Cloud Cover**: Patterns indicating trend changes.

### Trend Analysis
- Three types of trends:  
  - **Uptrend**: Higher highs and higher lows (HH and HL)
  - **Downtrend**: Lower highs and lower lows (LH and LL)
  - **Sideways (consolidation)**: Price moves within a range with no clear direction.
- Identification: Draw **trend lines** connecting successive highs/lows.
- Breaks in trendlines often signal reversals or continuations.

---

### Support and Resistance
- **Support**: Price level where buying interest causes bounce-back upwards.
- **Resistance**: Price level where selling pressure causes price to decline.
- These levels act like floors and ceilings, guiding trade decisions.
- When price breaks resistance and retests it, resistance turns support and vice versa.

---

### Indicators Overview
- Indicators help confirm price trends and trade signals.
- Four main indicator types:
  1. **Trend Indicators** (e.g., Moving Averages)  
     - *Simple Moving Average (SMA)*: Equal weights to past data, slower reaction.  
     - *Weighted Moving Average (WMA)*: More weight to recent data, quicker reaction.  
     - *Exponential Moving Average (EMA)*: Combination of SMA and WMA properties.
  2. **Momentum Indicators** (e.g., RSI - Relative Strength Index)  
     - Measures speed and strength of price movement.  
     - RSI below 30 = Oversold (potential buy), above 70 = Overbought (potential sell).
  3. **Volatility Indicators** (e.g., Bollinger Bands)  
     - Measure price fluctuation range.
  4. **Volume Indicators** (e.g., Volume-based moving averages)  
     - Assess trading activity strength; confirms price moves.

### Order Types and Trading Mechanics
- Orders facilitate buying/selling shares:
  - **Market Order**: Executes immediately at best available price.
  - **Limit Order**: Executes only at specified or better price.
  - **Stop Loss (SL) Order**: Automatically sells when price hits predefined level, limiting losses.
  - **GTD Order**: Good Till Date; order stays active till specified date or trigger.
- Trade duration types:
  - **Scalpers**: Very short-term trades (minutes).
  - **Intraday Traders**: Buy-sell within same trading day.
  - **Swing Traders**: Hold for days to weeks.
  - **Long-term Investors**: Hold for months to years.

### Risk Management & Psychology
- Crucial to manage risk strategically rather than investing all capital in one stock.
- Diversify portfolio across stocks and sectors (small, mid, large-cap stocks).
- Maintain a **risk-to-reward ratio** ideally between $1:1.5$ to $1:2$, meaning potential reward outweighs risk.
- Discipline, patience, and detachment from emotional biases are fundamental to successful trading.
- Practice through **paper trading** and **backtesting** strategies reduces mistakes and builds confidence.

---

### Practical Steps for Beginners
- Open a Demat and trading account via a reputed broker using PAN and Aadhaar verification.
- Link bank account for seamless fund transfer.
- Fund your trading account to start buying shares online.
- Analyze stocks using fundamentals and technical methods before investing.
- Use charts, trendlines, and indicators to confirm entry and exit points.
- Start small, progressively increasing exposure as comfort builds.
- Continuously learn and adapt trading style to personal preferences.
